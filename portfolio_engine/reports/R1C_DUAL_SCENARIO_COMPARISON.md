## 1. The scenario (a defined sensitivity, not a new truth)

| Part | Rule |
|---|---|
| NIFTY | numeric trade id (a per-contract, per-IST-day non-decreasing counter; meaning undocumented) |
| SENSEX | nanosecond time embedded in the trade id, when it agrees with the raw epoch (round-half-up); otherwise the epoch-derived time (the exceptional row 8079) |
| final tie-break | source-file row order |
| not used | the SENSEX id suffix; the order number |
| scope | only fills of the same instrument inside the same second are reordered; seconds themselves are never reordered |
| implementation | policy `per_contract_id_sequence`, Python only, sensitivity-only; convention id `SENSITIVITY_ONLY:per_contract_id_sequence`; the delivered-output builders refuse it (`require_primary`) |

### Distinct from the individual policies (measured, not assumed)

Same-instrument-second groups with at least two fills: 4,464. Groups where the hybrid order differs from the other policy's order:

| Other policy | Groups where the orders differ | Where |
|---|---:|---|
| `source_row` | 172 | all |
| `trade_id` | 2 | SENSEX 2 |
| `order_no` | 35 | NIFTY 18, SENSEX 17 |
| `sensex_trade_id_time` | 151 | NIFTY 151 |

The hybrid is **not** `trade_id` (that policy orders SENSEX by the whole id including the suffix, so fills sharing one nanosecond are ordered by suffix; here that separates the two policies in the two SENSEX groups shown in the JSON examples, rows 4736-4744 and 4745-4747), **not** `order_no`, and **not** `sensex_trade_id_time` (that policy leaves NIFTY in source-row order). On this ledger it happens to produce the same accounting outcome as `trade_id` (below); that is a measured coincidence of this data set, not an equivalence, and the policies stay separate.

## 5. Which conclusions are stable and which depend on the ordering

### Stable under both scenarios (tested here)

* Fill count, aggregate opening and closing units, final inventory per instrument, cash flow, FIFO and LIFO realized P&L totals, total episode count.
* Closing-batch **counts** at every threshold (membership differs by one batch per view).
* W3 links (0 changed); W1/W2 link counts change by 6 of 42,777 and the S60 views by 6 of 65,228.

### Dependent on the ordering convention (tested here)

* Position (episode) identity for 4 episodes in 2 instruments, and the reversal-ended episode count (23 vs 21).
* Opening/closing allocation of 4 fills.
* FIFO and LIFO lot attribution of 666 / 662 closing fills (3.7 to 3.8 % of 17,760 closing fills): any analysis that depends on **which lot** a close consumed must be run under both.
* Membership of one closing batch per view; 6 W1/W2 links and 6 S60 links; opening body units (626,765 baseline vs 625,985).

### Not tested here (UNKNOWN, not "stable")

* The M2-D evidence-class labels (W1 WEAKLY_SUPPORTED, W1_S60/W3 UNRESOLVED, W2, ownership, protective intent) were **not re-derived** under the hybrid ordering. Only link and item counts were compared; the small count changes make a label change unlikely but that is an inference, not a result.
* M2-E case studies, figures and records were not regenerated (by decision); their dependence on the ordering was **not measured**; it can only arise through the quantities listed as dependent above (an expectation, not a test).
* Any M3 result: none exists.

## 6. Rules for downstream research (project decision Option C)

1. The baseline stays `SOURCE_ROW_ORDER_CONVENTION`; delivered M2 outputs are never regenerated or replaced.
2. Every downstream research result is reported under **both** orderings, side by side, with both result sets stored.
3. A conclusion is called ordering-stable only if it holds under both; otherwise it is labelled ordering-dependent with the size of the dependence.
4. Neither ordering is described as verified chronology; the individual `trade_id`, `order_no`, `sensex_trade_id_time` policies remain separate sensitivities.
5. Source data is never modified; the ordering is a configurable convention applied at replay time (M3 requirement, see `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md`).
