## Question 2 — true margin
**M1. Margin rule used** (as in `PREMIUM_AND_ARCHITECTURE_PLAN.md`): 16-scenario SPAN-style risk + 2% extreme-loss margin on short notional (+2% on expiry day for cycles from 20 Nov 2024); price scan range per cycle = max(6·√2·σ_daily, 9.3%) with σ_daily = D-1 opening ATM implied volatility ÷ √250; volatility scan = max(25% of that implied volatility, 4 volatility points) (alternative reading: 4 points flat; reported both).
**M2. Real book.** Rebuild the real position of the 35 real cycles from the 39,874 real orders (every order carries its filled lots; 3 partial fills), at every 15th minute of both days and the D-1 close, valued on the same option chains. Per cycle: real peak margin and the share of minutes above ₹2 crore; compare with the simulator's full-strategy peak margin on the same cycles (Spearman, ratio of medians, week-cluster bootstrap).
Reading rules: the margin model is **consistent with a binding ₹2 crore constraint** if the real peak margin has median between ₹1.0 and ₹2.0 crore and exceeds ₹2 crore in at most 10% of cycles; it **overstates** if the real peak is typically above ₹2 crore; **capital is not the binding constraint** if the real peak margin is typically below ₹1 crore. Model–real agreement requires Spearman ≥ 0.6 over cycles. Anything else is reported as "not validated".
**M3. Component necessity at the verified-best scan range.** Repeat the earlier margin table with the per-cycle rule above (expected to equal the 9.3% column except in high-volatility cycles; I report the share of cycles where 6·√2·σ exceeds 9.3%).

## Question 3 — extreme gaps
**G1. Stress on actual books.** Gap grid ±3, ±5, ±7, ±10, ±13, ±15% × implied-volatility multiplier 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, applied at the D-1 close to (i) the 275 simulated carried books (full strategy, and the same without D-1 longs = "no wings" and without the lump), (ii) the 32 real carried books (and the real book with its long legs removed). Loss in ₹ and as % of ₹2 crore. Reading rule: a protection **prevents ruin** if the book with it loses less than ₹2 crore at the largest gap in the documented history (13%, IV × 2) while the book without it loses more.
**G2. Gap frequency.** (a) Overnight gaps ln(open/previous close) of the repo's daily NIFTY and SENSEX files (≈ 770 sessions each): maximum, quantiles, exceedances. (b) A generalised Pareto fit to |gap| above the 95th percentile (maximum likelihood, shape bounded in [−0.5, 1]), with 2,000 bootstrap draws, giving P(|gap| ≥ 3, 5, 7, 10%) per night; exponential-tail (shape 0) and shape 0.3 as sensitivities. (c) **Documented history:** the number of NIFTY close-to-close falls ≥ 5% from the two published lists (plus 4 Jun 2024 from a news search) over 2000–2026. These are **upper bounds** for the corresponding overnight gaps only if a gap is not larger than the day's move, which is not guaranteed; stated as a rough comparator.
**G3. Do the wings pay in expectation?** Break-even per night = (net cost of the longs per cycle) ÷ (mean extra loss without them at gap g) for g = 3, 5, 7, 10%. Expected benefit per cycle = Σ over gap bins of P(gap in bin) × (loss without − loss with), under (i) the 3-year empirical gaps, (ii) the GPD fit, (iii) the documented large events with the gap set to the full close-to-close move (upper bound) and to half of it, plus the in-sample distribution below 5%. Reading rule: wings **pay in expectation** only if the expected benefit exceeds the cost under the GPD fit **and** the documented comparator; **survival insurance only** if they fail on expectation but prevent ruin (G1).

## Question 1 — why is expiry-day premium rich?
Outcome variable: realised ÷ implied variance on D0 (`DESIGN_THESIS_PLAN.md` definitions), and short-straddle P&L as % of premium. Settlement price = the official close (the repo's daily NIFTY/SENSEX close of the D0 date; the repo's earlier validation compared minute data with these daily files).
**E1. Settlement averaging.** NSE index closing values are built from constituent closing prices; if those are last-30-minute averages, the settlement variable has less variance in the last half hour than a spot print. Test: Var[ln(settle ÷ spot at 15:00)] ÷ Var[ln(spot 15:29 close ÷ spot at 15:00)] over the 275 cycles (a Brownian average over 30 minutes predicts about 1/3; no averaging predicts 1), and the short-straddle P&L from 15:00 to settlement versus to the 15:29 print. I do **not** verify NSE's closing-price rule here; the test measures the effect whatever its cause.
**E2. Pinning.** At 14:00 on D0 find the strike with the largest total open interest within ±2% of spot; statistic = |settle − that strike| in strike steps, versus the same statistic using the max-OI strike of another cycle of the same index (200 placebo draws per cycle, ratio distribution) — pinning predicts a smaller observed distance (p < 0.05, one-sided).
**E3. Moneyness (tail/lottery demand vs uniform over-pricing).** At the D0 09:16 open, for puts at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3% below spot and calls at the same distances above (and ATM): mean price versus mean payoff at settlement; ratio price ÷ payoff and difference as % of the ATM straddle premium; 95% week-cluster bootstrap. Lottery/tail demand predicts the ratio **rising with distance**; uniform over-pricing predicts a flat ratio; hedging demand predicts puts richer than calls.
**E4. Option order-flow proxy.** On D0, per 15-minute block: signed volume = Σ over the ATM call and put of sign(price change in the minute) × volume (tick rule); regress the **next** block's change in implied total variance of the ATM straddle on this block's signed volume and on the squared index return of the next block, with week-cluster errors. A significant negative coefficient on signed volume (price pressure that reverses) is evidence of demand pressure; the proxy is crude (no quotes) and the result is labelled **weak evidence** whichever way it falls.
**E5. Decomposition.** Share of the D0 premium (1 − ratio, and the P&L) attributable to E1 (last 30 minutes) versus the rest; premium before 14:00 reported separately.
Reading rules per test: **supported** if the predicted sign appears with the 95% interval excluding the null; **weak** if the sign is right but the interval includes the null; **not supported** otherwise. A remaining unexplained premium after E1–E5 is reported as unexplained, not assigned to demand.
